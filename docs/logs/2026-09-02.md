writers and `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` holds the write lock. Safety comes from the
transaction boundary, not the allocator SQL. Documented in the suite.

# Session 2 — Phase 6 design & research

**Phase:** 6 (design stage) · **Code written:** NONE

## Scope correction
The roadmap says P6 delivers "18 groups, 23 ledgers". Measured: **P2 already
provisions 18 groups, 4 ledgers, 8 voucher types.** The 23 figure is the
*reference's* count, not a P6 requirement — P2-1 deliberately keeps module
ledgers (GST, Purchase Accounts) out of provisioning so a build without the
module cannot show an account nothing can post to. P6 must not undo that.

So P6 is governance, lifecycle and the trial balance — not "write the CoA".

## Four reference defects found (all new this session)
- **R-1** `isSystem` is set at provisioning and **never checked** — 0 occurrences
  across both ledger routes.
- **R-2** A system ledger with no postings is **hard-deletable**. On a fresh
  company that is every one of them; the failure surfaces later when a module
  resolves it.
- **R-3** PATCH passes `validatedData` straight to `update`, including
  `groupId`. Moving a ledger across natures flips `isDebitNatured`
  (`posting.ts:417`), so future postings move the balance the opposite way from
  the accumulated history.
- **R-4** `post-invoice.ts:207` resolves Round Off **by display name** via
  `getOrCreateNamedLedger`. Renaming it creates a *second* control account and
  splits the balance. Our `UNIQUE (system_code)` already prevents this.

## Gap in our own schema
`ledger_group` has `is_system`; **`ledger` does not**. Recorded as P6-1.

## Adversarial review — 6 findings, one High
**A-9 (High):** the design covered ledger lifecycle and said nothing about
**group** deletion. Deleting a group with children would orphan them, and P2
measured that `foreign_keys` defaults OFF per connection, so the FK is not a
reliable backstop. Rules added.

Also: A-6 deactivating a ledger with a balance must keep it in the trial balance
(hiding it would unbalance the books); A-10 a cross-year reversal correctly
leaves FY1 not netting to zero — right, but must be tested rather than
discovered.

## B-1 traced, as instructed
**B-1 does not materially change the P6 kernel.** Because P2-1 keeps module
ledgers out of provisioning, the accounts whose existence depends on B-1 — COGS,
Purchase Accounts, Inventory Adjustment — are not P6's to create. The one thing
P6 must avoid is seeding a COGS ledger "for completeness", which would silently
commit to perpetual. **Recommend proceeding; do not elevate B-1.**
